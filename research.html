On the Typical Geometry of Quant-Finance Manifolds:
When PCA Remains Competitive with Diffusion Maps
(and When Diffusion Maps Offer a Superior Encoding)

Leo Rasmussen · Independent Study with Prof. James Meiss · July 2026
University of Colorado Boulder · Applied Mathematics

Download PDF ← Back to home

Abstract

Principal component analysis (PCA) remains the default linear dimensionality-reduction technique in quantitative finance. Diffusion maps were designed to recover intrinsic geometry on curved manifolds and therefore appear, a priori, better suited to many financial data sets. We prove a finite-sample principal-angle bound showing that, once residual curvature is only mild and the diffusion bandwidth is not extremely small, the leading diffusion subspace collapses toward the PCA score subspace. Consequently any remaining performance difference is statistical rather than geometric.

Empirically the dichotomy is sharp. After a standard Dynamic Nelson–Siegel fit, residual U.S. Treasury yield curves exhibit low curvature and large data-driven bandwidth; diffusion maps cannot improve on PCA. On raw SPY implied-volatility surfaces the curvature proxy is markedly higher and the bandwidth smaller; diffusion maps reduce out-of-sample reconstruction error by 30–55% relative to PCA at every rank examined. The winner is therefore determined by the geometry of the cloud under study and by the scale at which the diffusion kernel operates.

Key Theoretical Result

Under mild curvature and sufficiently large bandwidth, the diffusion-map score subspace and the PCA score subspace become close. The principal-angle bound is:

$$\|\sin\Theta(V_\varepsilon, V_{\mathrm{PCA}})\|_{\mathrm{op}} \le C\Bigl(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^2} + \kappa\delta\Bigr)$$

where $\varepsilon$ is the diffusion bandwidth, $\kappa$ bounds residual curvature (second fundamental form), $\delta$ is the tubular radius around a linear subspace, and $C$ depends on the spectral gap and cloud diameter. When the right-hand side is small, diffusion maps cannot extract directions materially different from PCA.

Empirical Summary

Treasury yield residuals (after DNS)

Low curvature · Large bandwidth

After Dynamic Nelson–Siegel, residual curvature is modest ($\mathrm{curv} \approx 0.08$–$0.21$) and the median-heuristic bandwidth is large relative to residual scale. Diffusion maps and PCA produce essentially identical full-curve reconstruction errors (ratios 1.01–1.12). Robustness properties of PCA dominate.

SPY implied-volatility surfaces

Higher curvature · Smaller bandwidth

Raw surfaces show elevated curvature proxy ($\mathrm{curv} \approx 0.26$–$0.44$). Diffusion maps reduce out-of-sample relative Frobenius reconstruction error by 30–55% versus PCA at every rank $d=1\dots5$. Clear geometric advantage.

Interpretation

The large-bandwidth analysis explains why diffusion maps lose their advantage precisely on residual manifolds that remain after standard linear or parametric factor models. On those manifolds the leading diffusion coordinates collapse toward the PCA coordinates. Conversely, when the object of interest is the raw volatility surface itself, a moderate-bandwidth diffusion map recovers substantially more of the observed variance than a linear subspace of equal dimension.

Neither method is uniformly preferable. The winner is determined by the curvature of the cloud and the scale at which the diffusion kernel operates.
